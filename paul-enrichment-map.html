0The New York state file goes first

Four USBizData New York files, bought once and kept in your own database. Every owner in your book is matched against them before anything is traced. Each owner the file reaches is one less trace to pay for.

15,801,605Residential: name, address, phone, age, years at address
5,141,785Cell phones
7,297,939Personal email
5,514,091Business: company, contact, title, direct phone, email

Each owner gets one of four reach codes. The code decides the next step:

R1
The owner, found by nameMatched at the address the tax bill goes to (or at the property, for owners who live there). Phone, cell, email, age and years at the address come straight from the file.Lands in: Mobile 1–3 · Landline · Email 1–3 · Age · Years owned → ready to call
R2
Someone in the owner's householdA different name at the same address has a phone. Kept as a lead to the household, and the owner is also skip traced.Lands in: Household contact · then Path A, Pass 1
R3
The file can't reach themA person, trust or estate that isn't in the file. Goes straight to skip trace on your key.Next: Path A, Pass 1 (or Path C for an estate)
R4
A company owns itMatched against the business file by company name and mailing address: contact name, title, direct phone, email.Next: Path B

The file's phones are not verified. They get the same Do Not Call check as every other phone, and a wrong number moves the lead to its next-best phone. Once a traced owner gets a phone, they move from R3 up to R1.

Public mortgage records show whether a loan is adjustable, when it was recorded, its term and its maturity date. They don't show the exact reset date. That's why the 5-, 7- and 10-year test is labeled an estimate. No RealEstateAPI needed; the whole radar runs on your Apify key.
