🗄️ Data · where it comes from

🧠 Data engine
The system that finds the land, the owner and the contact info, and keeps all of it fresh every week.
🌊 Data lake
Your own database: every parcel in New York State, with everything we know joined onto it.
🧩 Parcel · lot
One piece of land on the county tax map.
🔢 Section-Block-Lot
The parcel's ID on the Long Island tax maps. It's how a lot is matched across every source.
📜 Owner of record
The name on the tax roll: the legal owner, even when it's an LLC or a trust.
🏛️ NYS tax parcels
New York State's public file of every parcel: owner, mailing address, acres, value, deed book and page.
🏙️ NYC lot file (PLUTO)
New York City's public file of every lot in the five boroughs: zoning, building size, how much more can be built.
🌐 Open data
Public government data. It costs $0 and anyone can check it.
🏡 OneKey MLS
The multiple listing service for Long Island and the NY metro: every listing, expired, withdrawn and sold.
📦 USBizData bundle
Bought data files for New York State: residents (age, income, years at the address), cell phones, emails and businesses.
🔍 Tracerfy
The skip-trace service: it finds an owner's phones and emails and checks them against Do Not Call.
🏦 RealEstateAPI
Optional property data service: loans, adjustable-rate resets, equity, years owned.
🔄 Apify
The tool that re-checks your listings every week, so the book stays current.
🛰️ Mapbox
The satellite map behind every lot.
📸 Mapillary
Street-level photos: the front of the property.
🔑 API key
A password that lets your engine use your account at a service like Tracerfy or SignalHouse. It stays in your name and on your bill.
🪙 Credits
What a data service charges per record. Counting a market costs 0 credits; pulling the records costs credits.
🧹 Weekly sweep
Every week we re-check Suffolk and Nassau for new expireds, listings back on the market, new deeds and building permits.

🎯 Signals · the reason to call now

⚡ Event signal
Something that just happened that gives an owner a reason to sell now.
⏳ MLS expired
A listing that ended without selling.
🚫 Never relisted or sold
It expired and never came back on the market. The owner may still want out.
🗓️ Aged expired
Expired between 91 days and 2 years ago. Other agents have stopped calling.
🔁 Expired 2+ times
Listed and failed more than once. A motivated owner.
🐢 Long on market
Sat for a long time before it expired (high days on market).
📅 Days on market (DOM)
How long a listing was up before it sold, expired or came down.
⚖️ Estate · trust
An estate or a trust owns it. Often heirs who'd rather sell.
🏗️ Developers buying nearby
Companies have been taking title to land around it. Proof there's demand.
✈️ Absentee owner
The owner doesn't live at the property.
🗺️ Out-of-state owner
The owner lives in another state.
🌾 Vacant land
No building on the lot.
🏘️ Holds more parcels
The same owner has other lots. One call can open several deals.
⚠️ Adjustable-rate reset
The owner's loan rate is about to change. Optional, from RealEstateAPI.
🧺 Segment
A slice of the book that shares a signal or a size, like "aged expireds, 2–5 acres".
🔕 No foreclosure pulls
Your book does not pull pre-foreclosure, lis pendens or auction lists.

📐 The deal · what the land can become

📏 Acres · frontage · depth
How big the lot is, how wide it is on the road, and how deep it runs.
🏷️ Zoning
The town's rules for what can be built and the smallest lot allowed.
✂️ Lots by right
How many lots the land can be split into under today's zoning, with no variance.
💧 Septic rule
Suffolk County's sanitary code sets a minimum lot size where there's no sewer. It can cap the split.
🔎 Split candidate
Big enough to split, but the exact lot count needs the town's zoning checked.
🏢 Buildable floor area
In NYC: how much building the zoning allows on the lot, and how much is still unused.
💵 Last asking
The price on the last listing before it expired.
📈 Permit trend
How many new homes the town has permitted each year. Rising means builders are active.
⭐ Deal potential
The deal read in one card: acres, lots by right, last asking, who's building nearby, the next move.

📱 Reach + ☎️ click to call · getting to the owner

🕵️ Skip trace
Finding an owner's phones and emails from their name and address.
🎯 Hit · miss
A hit found contact info (you pay 2¢). A miss found nothing (you pay nothing).
🏷️ Line type
Whether a number is a mobile, a landline or an internet line. Only mobiles get texts.
🥇 Ranked phones
The owner's numbers in the order to try them, best first.
🚫 Do Not Call (DNC)
The national list of numbers that don't want sales calls. DNC numbers get no Call button.
✋ STOP
An owner who replies STOP is blocked from every text and call after that.
❌ Wrong number
The number doesn't reach the owner. It's blocked so nobody tries it again.
✔️ Verified
The number or email was checked and is live.
☎️ Click to call
One click dials the owner from your own number, right from the lot. The call pad opens on the lead. This is where the data engine ends.
📲 Your own line
Phone numbers in your SignalHouse account, in your name.

✅ Call outcomes · what happened on the call

📵 No answer · voicemail
Nobody picked up, or you left a message. It comes back around for another try.
🔙 Callback
The owner asked to talk later. The date goes on the lot.
🏠 Keep · 💰 Sell
What the owner said they want to do with the land.
🔥 OPPT (opportunity)
A real deal in play. Only a person marks it; the system never does.
📅 APPT (appointment)
A meeting is booked.

🧾 The record · what the data engine keeps

🧾 Outreach ledger
The record of every call, text and email, kept on the lot. It lives in the data engine so each record is complete. Keeping the record isn't the same as sending: email and texts are the Campaign Engine.
✍️ Attempt
One line in the ledger: a call, text or email, with the time, the line and the outcome.
🗒️ Notes
What your team wrote on the lot. Every note stays with the owner's record.
🧱 Block
A list of leads, best first: your team calls it from the data engine, or hands it to the Campaign Engine. The unit your team works through.
📇 One record
Land, listing, owner, person, phones, emails and the ledger, all on one lot.
🚀 CAMPAIGN ENGINEIts own package: email and texts to whole lists, replies, follow-up and appointments. Wherever you launch your first campaign, we've got you.

💬 Campaign · email, texts and replies

🚀 Campaign Engine
The part that sends: email and texts to whole lists, replies, follow-up, and appointments into your CRM. It starts where click to call ends.
✉️ Email
Emails to owners from your own address. Every email is logged on the lot.
💬 First text
The first text an owner gets from you (we call it the Initial SMS). One approved message to a whole block.
👆 Send
The button a person presses. Nothing goes out until someone presses it, and one press covers one block.
📝 Brand + campaign registration
Carriers make every business that texts register who it is and what it sends (called 10DLC). Texting starts once it's approved.
🏅 Brand vetting
An extra carrier check that raises how many texts a day you're allowed.
🔥 Warm-up
A new number starts slow (250 texts a day) and builds to 500 by day 4, so carriers trust it.
📬 Delivered
The carrier confirmed the text reached the phone.
🙋 Reply
Any answer from an owner is a raised hand. Replies get worked first.
🌱 Nurture
Slower follow-up for owners who aren't ready yet.
🤝 Follow Up Boss
Your CRM. Booked appointments land there on their own.
