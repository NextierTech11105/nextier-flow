How a search works. Explained like a filing cabinet.

Your data lake is a Postgres database: one very large, very organized filing cabinet. Here's everything that happens, from the moment the files arrive to the moment you type a town and the right lots come up.

📥 Files arrive→🧽 Cleaned + labeled→🔗 Owners matched→🔍 Gaps traced→🏆 Scored + ranked→🔎 You search
Part 1

🗄️ The cabinet has three drawers

Every record moves from left to right, one drawer at a time.

📥 Drawer 1 · Raw
"bronze"
The files exactly as they arrive: the state tax roll, your USBizData bundle, your MLS sweep, Tracerfy results. Never edited, so any number can be checked back to where it came from.
🧽 Drawer 2 · Cleaned
"silver"
The same records, rewritten the same way so they can be matched: one row per parcel, one row per person, every address and name labeled with a key.
🏆 Drawer 3 · Ready
"gold"
Your targets: matched, sorted, scored and ranked. This is the drawer your search opens.
Part 2

🏷️ Labels make matching possible

Two lists rarely write the same thing the same way. So every address and every name gets a simple label, a "key", written one way only.

🏠 The address key
128 Pauls Path, Coram 11727→128|PAULS|11727
Crow Lane · CROW LA · Crow Ln→CROW
84-74 Main St→8474
House number, street name, ZIP. Suffixes like Lane or Street are dropped, so spelling can't break a match.
👤 The name key
SMITH, JOHN (tax roll)→SMITH
John Smith (USBizData)→SMITH
O'Brien · Van Der Berg→OBRIEN · VANDERBERG
Smith Family Trust→SMITH
The surname, letters only. Now the tax roll and your bundle speak the same language.
Part 3

🔗 Matching each owner to your bundle

For every parcel, Postgres takes the owner's name key and the address their tax bill goes to, and looks for that same pair in your USBizData files. Every owner lands in one of four buckets.

✅ R1
Found the owner
Their phone, cell, email, age and years at the address come straight from your bundle.
🏠 R2
Found the household
Someone else living with the owner has a phone.
🔍 R3
Not reachable yet
Goes to the Tracerfy line, best first, on your key at 2¢ a hit. When a phone comes back, the owner moves up to R1.
🏢 R4
A company owns it
Matched in the business file, then a person finds who signs.
Your bundle goes first because you already own it. Tracerfy only runs on R3, which keeps the skip-trace bill small. Town, state and utility owners are counted but never targeted.
Part 4

🏆 Sorting and ranking

Now every target gets three things, using simple rules anyone can check.

  1. One segmentThe first rule a parcel fits, in a fixed order: land that can be split, homes on big lots, redevelopment sites, and so on.
  2. A score from 0 to 100Owner first (individual owners rank highest), then the land, then the reasons to move, then whether you can reach them.
  3. A tier, P1 to P4, and a rankP1 is the best. Inside each segment, lots are ranked by score, then by size.
Part 5

📑 Tabs make it fast

Your bundle alone holds 15.8 million residents. Without help, finding one person means reading every one. An index works like the tabs in a binder or the index at the back of a book: Postgres flips straight to the right page.

County + townAddress + last nameParcel IDSegment + tier + scoreReach (R1–R4)Company name
Part 6 · the moment you search

🔎 You type "Brookhaven". Here's what happens.

  1. You type the town and press Enter.The same box takes a county, a town, a ZIP or an owner's name.
  2. The page asks Postgres one question."Give me the targets in Suffolk County, town of Brookhaven, best first."
  3. Postgres flips to the county + town tab.It goes straight to Brookhaven and never reads the rest of the state.
  4. It sorts those lots.Tier first, then score: P1 on top.
  5. It hands back the first page.Address, acres, segment, tier, score: your list view.
  6. You click a lot.Postgres pulls that one parcel by its ID: land, owner, listing, matched person, phones, and the record of every touch. That's your universal lead card.
🤓 For the curious, the actual question:
select site_addr, acres, segment, tier, score
from gold.targets
where county = 'Suffolk' and town = 'Brookhaven'
order by tier, score desc
limit 50;
Part 7

🔄 Every Monday, the cabinet updates itself

The weekly sweep re-checks your listings and writes down what changed.

🟢 Expired, never relistedGoes to the top of your book.
🔴 Newly listedTaken out of outreach. They have an agent.
⚫ SoldOut of the book. The price becomes a comp.
🔥 For sale by ownerA person calls the same day.
🏗️ Builder bought next doorThe lot gets a neighbor flag.
🧾 The countsEvery number on your pages comes from one counting step, so any number traces back to its rows.
Part 8

🌊 Where the cabinet lives: DigitalOcean

Your data lake runs in your own DigitalOcean account. Each piece has one job.

🗄️ Managed PostgreSQL · the cabinetYour database. DigitalOcean runs the machine; we run what's inside. It supports PostGIS for the parcel shapes and the name-matching tools (pg_trgm, fuzzystrmatch).
📦 Spaces · the storage unitEvery original file as it arrived: the tax roll, your bundle, each Monday sweep, every Tracerfy result. $5 a month holds 250 GB.
🧑‍💼 Droplet · the clerkA small server that loads new files, runs the Monday sweep and prepares the Tracerfy list.
🌙 Backups · a copy every nightA full backup every day, and you can rewind to any moment in the last 7 days.
🛟 Standby node · a second cabinetIf the main database fails, the standby takes over on its own.
📖 Read-only node · a copy for reportsBig counts and reports run on the copy, so search never waits.
🔔 Alerts · a smoke detectorDigitalOcean warns us when the database gets busy or full, before you'd notice.
⬆️ Scale up · a bigger cabinetMove to a bigger plan whenever the book grows. Your data comes with it.
